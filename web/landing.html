A bust is three failures at once

"The forecast was wrong" is not a usable label: every forecast is wrong by some margin. A bust is a failure that would have changed what a forecaster did, so all three of these have to be true together.

  • Magnitude

    The error is at least 10 mm/day, or the subdivision's own 95th-percentile training error for that month, whichever is larger.

  • Category flip

    Forecast and observation land in different IMD intensity bands. The forecast said one thing; the day was another.

  • Operational significance

    At least one side reaches 15.6 mm/day. A dry-day error nobody would have acted on is not a bust.

That conjunction is why the base rate is about 4% rather than "most days". It is a rare-event problem by construction.

Unknown is not safe

When the atmospheric state is unlike anything in training, the system returns OUT_OF_DISTRIBUTION and no probability. The obvious worry is that this is an excuse. It is not.

On the held-out test year, the days the model declined to score busted (loading) more often than the days it accepted. A refusal is elevated, unknown risk: never a quiet result, and never drawn as one.

That is why there are three review tiers and not two. Folding refusals into "not flagged" would hide the highest-risk days in the product.
