Definition

Three conditions, all of which must hold

"The forecast was wrong" is not a usable label — every forecast is wrong by some margin. A bust is a failure that would have changed what a forecaster did, so all three of these have to be true at once.

1  Magnitude

The error is at least 10 mm/day, or the subdivision's own 95th-percentile training error for that month — whichever is larger.

2  Category flip

Forecast and observation land in different IMD intensity bands. The forecast said one thing; the day was another.

3  Operational significance

At least one side reaches 15.6 mm/day. A dry-day error nobody would have acted on is not a bust.

That conjunction is why the base rate is about 4% rather than "most days". It is a rare-event problem by construction.

Refusal

Declining to score is a finding, not a gap

When the atmospheric state is unlike anything in training, the system returns OUT_OF_DISTRIBUTION and no probability. The obvious worry is that this is an excuse. It is not.

On the held-out 2022 test year, the days the model declined to score busted — more often than the days it accepted. A refusal is elevated, unknown risk — never a quiet result, and never shown as one.

This is why there are three escalation tiers and not two. Folding refusals into "not flagged" would hide the highest-risk days in the product.
